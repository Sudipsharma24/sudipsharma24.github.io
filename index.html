Sudip Sharma

PhD Candidate · Department of Energy and Process Engineering · NTNU, Trondheim, Norway

Trondheim, Norway

sudip.sharma@ntnu.no

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Energy and Process Engineering at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), supervised by Prof. Thomas A. Adams II. My research focuses on the electrification of post-combustion CO₂ capture through Magnetic Induction Swing Adsorption (MISA) — a temperature-swing adsorption concept in which the adsorbent bed is heated directly and rapidly by magnetic induction rather than by steam or hot purge gas. My PhD is funded through the NTNU–MIT Energy Research Programme (NMERP-C2).

I develop first-principles dynamic models of complete MISA cycles in IDAES/Pyomo, validated against published experimental data for magnetite–MOF composite adsorbents. On top of these models, I build Gaussian-process surrogates with active learning and apply multi-objective optimization (NSGA-II, MOPSO) to map purity–recovery and productivity–energy trade-offs across competing cycle designs. The simulation framework was introduced at ESCAPE-36, where the isotherm-fitting routines are released openly; the full multi-configuration cycle analysis is described in a journal manuscript currently under review, with its configuration code archived on LAPSE. I am now extending the framework to temperature- and vacuum-swing (TVSA/VSA) cycles for the same magnetite–MOF adsorbent, using batch Bayesian optimization (qNEHVI) for sample-efficient multi-objective design.

Before NTNU, I completed the BIOCEB European Master in Biological and Chemical Engineering for a Sustainable Bioeconomy (Erasmus Mundus), studying in France, Estonia, Belgium, and Austria, with my master’s thesis at TU Graz.

Research interests: process systems engineering · CO₂ capture · adsorption process modeling · surrogate modeling & active learning · multi-objective & Bayesian optimization
